Drimoland: DMX, dance, dragons

Drimoland is built for a theme-park venue: songs in the space, rig lighting in the room, and a build that has to stay in sync with what guests see and hear. The DMX path and the dance plugin exist so we can drive choreography and lighting from the same songs the park runs, instead of treating the floor and the screen as two unrelated shows.

Why we built it

The game is headed to a theme-park installation, not only a living-room screen. In the venue we already have songs and a lighting setup. We needed a system where what happens in the build can line up with what happens in the room: same cues, same moments, so the experience feels intentional instead of accidentally similar.

How it fits together

We author DMX and song-level choreography as part of production. That pipeline feeds the game and can drive what the venue rig does, so lighting and performance stay on the same rail. You can treat the park slice almost like a virtual experience tied to real hardware on site, not a separate light show bolted on afterward.

Park vs elsewhere

The full idea, songs plus room lighting plus screens, is aimed at the park. Outside the venue you might still hear the music in the product, but you do not get the same integrated lighting and space. The bridge matters most where the impressive rig actually lives.

Dragons, songs, kids

Content leans toward cute dragons that dance along to songs, aimed at kids (roughly ages 8–12, often younger) on theme-park screens. The track list is large (on the order of fifty songs and growing), so environments and lighting have to flex per song instead of one static look.
